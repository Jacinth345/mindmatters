What is Doom Scrolling?

Endless consumption of negative news

The tendency to keep scrolling through upsetting or alarming information online without stopping.

Compulsive scrolling despite distress

Continuing to browse negative content even when it causes anxiety, fear, or emotional exhaustion.

Anxiety-driven information seeking

The urge to check for updates or answers to ease uncertainty, which often fuels more stress instead.

Why is doom scrolling bad?

Increases anxiety and stress

Constant exposure to negative news keeps your mind in a state of worry and tension.

Disrupts sleep and focus

Late-night scrolling and over-stimulation make it hard to rest or concentrate during the day.

Leads to emotional exhaustion

Absorbing nonstop bad news drains your energy, leaving you feeling hopeless or overwhelmed.

What is Doom Scrolling?

Notice your triggers

Pay attention to when and why you tend to doomscroll. Are you anxious, bored, or avoiding something? Understanding your triggers is the first step to breaking the cycle.

Set limits

Schedule specific times to check news or social media, and limit how long you spend there. Use app timers or screen time settings to help you stick to your limits.

Reduce temptation

Turn off nonessential notifications and move social media apps off your home screen. Creating small barriers makes it easier to pause before opening them automatically.

Curate your content

Unfollow or mute accounts that post constant negativity. Instead, follow pages that share positive, educational, or creative content that uplifts or inspires you.

Replace the habit

When you feel the urge to scroll, switch to something that calms or energizes you—like journaling, reading, stretching, or taking a walk.

Replacing the habit makes it easier to stay off your phone long-term.
